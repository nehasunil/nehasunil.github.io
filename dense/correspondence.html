Reactive In-Air Clothing Manipulation with Confidence-Aware Dense Correspondence and Visuotactile Affordance


Neha Sunil*   Megha Tippur*   Arnau Saumell  

Edward H. Adelson   Alberto Rodriguez


MIT



Manipulating clothing is challenging due to complex configurations, material dynamics, and frequent self-occlusion. Prior systems often flatten garments or assume visibility of key features. We present a dual-arm visuotactile framework that combines confidence-aware dense visual correspondence and tactile-supervised grasp affordance to operate directly on crumpled and suspended garments. The correspondence model is trained on a custom, high-fidelity simulated dataset using a distributional loss that captures cloth symmetries and generates correspondence confidence estimates. These estimates guide a reactive state machine that adapts folding strategies based on perceptual uncertainty. In parallel, a visuotactile grasp affordance network, self-supervised using high-resolution tactile feedback, determines which regions are physically graspable. The same tactile classifier is used during execution for real-time grasp validation. By deferring action in low-confidence states, the system handles highly occluded table-top and in-air configurations. We demonstrate our task-agnostic grasp selection module in folding and hanging tasks. Moreover, our dense descriptors provide a reusable intermediate representation for other planning modalities, such as extracting grasp targets from human video demonstrations, paving the way for more generalizable and scalable garment manipulation.

System Overview

Our system enables reactive, confidence-aware, in-air garment manipulation directly from crumpled and suspended configurations, without a prior flattening step. By combining global visual perception with local tactile feedback, the robot can identify keypoints, adapt strategies in real time, and recover from failed grasps.

To enable this behavior, we have four main contributions (described further in below sections):

  1. Parametrizable Simulated Dataset. A custom dataset with realistic stitching features and parameterized variations to enable correspondences across different shirt geometries and material properties.
  2. Dense Representation. Pixel-wise correspondences across challenging states using a distributional loss to capture symmetries and provide confidence estimates.
  3. Visuotactile Affordance. Grasp affordance network trained in simulation and fine-tuned using tactile self-supervision to identify graspable regions with a single camera setup.
  4. Garment Manipulation System. A reactive visuotactile framework combining dense correspondences, affordances, and tactile sensing for confidence-aware in-air folding and hanging.

Parametrizable Simulated Dataset

We built a custom garment simulation pipeline in Blender 4.2, adding realistic hems, seams, and varied shirt geometries to better match real-world garments. By parameterizing shape, material properties, and camera setup, we generated 1,500 diverse hanging and table-top scenes with consistent vertex indexing, enabling pixel-wise correspondences between crumpled shirts and a canonical template.


Dense Representation

We train a ResNet34-based dense descriptor network to map each garment pixel to a canonical flat shirt. Unlike contrastive losses, which force a single “best match” and often fail on symmetric or occluded regions, our distributional loss models multiple valid correspondences and outputs a probability map.

This allows the network to:
  • Handles Symmetry: Distributional loss models multiple valid matches (e.g., either sleeve end), while contrastive forces a single match and often fails on symmetric regions.
  • Captures Ambiguity: Outputs a probability map over possible correspondences instead of a single “best” pixel, preserving spatial consistency in uncertain areas.
  • Confidence-Aware: Provides correspondence confidence for safer grasp planning, enabling the robot to defer low-certainty actions.

Visuotactile Affordance

To identify graspable garment regions, we train a depth-based affordance network in simulation and fine-tune it on the real robot using high-resolution tactile feedback. In simulation, ground-truth labels are computed from full cloth state to ensure reachable, collision-free grasps with minimal fabric layers.

On the robot, a tactile classifier distinguishes successful grasps from those with too little or excess fabric, providing self-supervised labels for fine-tuning. This visuotactile loop improves robustness to material variation and complex, crumpled configurations, enabling reliable grasp selection with a single camera view.




Garment Manipulation System

Our bimanual system combines dense correspondence, visuotactile affordance, and tactile sensing within a confidence-based state machine for in-air garment manipulation that dynamically chooses between grasp strategies. At each step, the robot selects grasp points where both correspondence and affordance exceed thresholds, deferring low-confidence actions and rotating the garment to expose new regions. Tactile feedback validates grasps in real time and enables closed-loop tensioning before folding or hanging. This reactive approach allows the robot to adapt strategies on the fly, recover from failures, and manipulate a wide range of garments without flattening.
